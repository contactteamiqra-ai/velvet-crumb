# All-chocolate asset direction — awaiting visual review

The final product is a premium, rich chocolate cake with **no strawberries or other fruit**. The current PR preview preserves the downloaded scan for review of camera, cutting and reverse scrolling. It is not the final product appearance. No asset adaptation or later production scenes begin before this visual review.

## Preserve realism while removing fruit

1. Keep the source scan and photographic atlas immutable. Work on a duplicate with the same physical scale and named intact/body/slice hierarchy.
2. Segment strawberry flesh, seeds and green leaves in both geometry and UV space. The source is a fused scan: fruit cannot safely be removed by deleting a named object or recoloring its red pixels brown. Inspect the exposed coverage before deciding how much of the scan remains useful.
3. Reconstruct the surface under the fruit from retained chocolate regions and reference-matched sculpture. Repair holes, remesh only the repaired regions, and maintain the scan's irregular frosting and real surface variation. Avoid smooth replacement cylinders or repeated procedural pores.
4. Author a deep chocolate finish with photographed/scanned ganache, rich cream and sponge detail. Keep base color free from baked lighting; match normal, roughness and displacement scale. Use restrained chocolate shavings or curls with natural thickness variation; no fruit-shaped chocolate substitutes.
5. Rebuild cut faces as three-dimensional chocolate sponge and cream layers, with photographed material references and a continuous ganache rim. Bake high-resolution surface detail into the web mesh. Match whole, body and slice textures so reversal leaves no seam.
6. Validate macro views under neutral and existing studio light. If fruit removal destroys too much usable scan surface, use a matching licensed chocolate-cake scan or commission one, retaining this scan as scale/material reference rather than pretending it remains photographic after reconstruction.
